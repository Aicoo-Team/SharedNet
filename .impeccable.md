## Design Context

### Users

Technical founders and product builders who can state an outcome but do not want to manually assemble, prompt, and supervise every specialist. In V1 they want to turn a rough website idea into a researched, built, deployed, and independently verified result. They should be able to understand the organization without operating it.

### Brand Personality

Decisive, procedural, alive. SharedNet should feel like a quiet control room in which a real organization forms around the work—not a chat toy, agent marketplace, or generic analytics dashboard. The emotional goal is earned confidence: the system asks what matters, exposes why it made each coordination decision, and never blurs simulated work with live external actions.

### Aesthetic Direction

An editorial mission dossier crossed with a network operations board: dark mineral ink, warm paper text, occasional signal-green state marks, compact operational metadata, and one asymmetrical workspace rather than a grid of interchangeable cards. Frontend weight stays light; the memorable object is the RAC task spine visibly opening as dependencies complete. Avoid neon cyberpunk, glassmorphism, purple-blue gradients, chat bubbles, KPI dashboards, and fake terminal-token streams.

### Design Principles

1. Lead with the outcome; reveal the Agent organization only when it becomes useful.
2. Show causality, not activity theater: every Agent, edge, wait state, and event has a reason.
3. Preserve truth boundaries: SIMULATED and LIVE are persistent, unmistakable labels.
4. Ask one consequential question at a time and keep readiness visible without turning it into a form marathon.
5. Make the final handoff tangible through preview, files, manifests, and independent evidence.
6. Preserve keyboard access, visible focus, readable contrast, and reduced-motion behavior in every stage.
