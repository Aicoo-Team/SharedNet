## Design Context

### Users

Technical founders and operators who want to state an outcome once and let a network of owned and trusted external Agents organize the work. They need to understand who is acting, whose authority is being used, what requires a decision, and what the work costs—without manually orchestrating every worker.

### Brand Personality

Quiet, capable, and legible. SharedNet should feel like a thin command surface over a serious coordination system: the product stays out of the way until a network relationship, authorization, or decision matters. It is not an agent marketplace, workflow builder, or activity dashboard.

### Aesthetic Direction

A warm, daylight workspace with dark ink, restrained green signal color, and precise operational typography. The primary surface is a sparse typing interface. Network topology and decisions use borders, labels, spacing, and linework instead of card grids or decoration. Avoid gradients, glass, chat bubbles, KPI dashboards, neon cyberpunk styling, oversized hero copy, and fake terminal streams.

### Design Principles

1. One prompt is the front door; planning happens in the conversation.
2. Make Principal ownership and cross-Principal boundaries unmistakable.
3. Interrupt only for decisions that require human authority.
4. Show costs as a platform-wide ledger, not isolated model trivia.
5. Preserve truth boundaries: demo Agents and simulated execution are always labeled.
6. Keep all primary navigation to Chat, Network, and Decisions.
7. Preserve keyboard access, visible focus, readable contrast, 44px targets, and reduced-motion behavior.
